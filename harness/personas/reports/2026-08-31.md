Triage discipline reminder: complaints are **leads, not verdicts**. Every item
below was verified against the journey log before ranking. CONFIRMED = the
logged response demonstrably does what the complaint says. PERSONA-OPINION =
taste/temperament, or the app could not have known.

## Ranked issues

### 1. CONFIRMED — Time budget is aspirational, not computed (S1, blocks Priya's goal)
> Priya: "I timed it: with your suggested sets and any realistic rest between
> them, this runs way over 30 minutes. Time is my hard stop."

Evidence: generate returned **"30-Minute Quiet Full Body Circuit"** with 6
exercises / 16 work sets (3+3+3+3+2+2). At ~45 s work + 60–90 s rest that is
28–42 minutes of sets alone, before warm-up — the title promises 30, the
prescription can't deliver it. The revise call did trim to 8 sets (good), but
only after the user complained.
**Engineering read:** the generator never computes duration (sets × work+rest)
against `defaultTimeLimitMinutes`; the time limit rides along as prose, so the
model decorates the title with it instead of constraining volume.

### 2. CONFIRMED — Adaptation is reactive-only; pain history never acts on the routine (S1, Marcus, the M4 gap)
> Marcus: "I wish the app had caught this earlier instead of making me deal
> with ongoing pain first."

Evidence: Marcus's 3-week memory digest carried 7 dated pain reports and
"too hard" check-ins. When *asked* ("what should I do next?"), the coach read
it perfectly: "your shoulder pain has been persistent… cut the number of work
sets on pressing by one third and drop lateral raises entirely." But (a)
nothing surfaced this until the user asked, and (b) the excellent advice came
back `action=reply_only` — his saved routine still contains the lateral raises
the coach just told him to drop.
**Engineering read:** this is M4 by another name. The reactive half already
works (the digest + coach do their jobs); missing pieces are a proactive
check-in→suggestion trigger and the coach preferring `updated_draft` when its
own advice implies concrete routine edits.

### 3. CONFIRMED — Generator consistency bugs: duplicate exercise, count ignores "very simple" (S2, Dana)
> Dana: "The first routine had too many exercises — seven plus treadmill —
> and that felt overwhelming for a total beginner like me."

Evidence: Dana asked for "very simple"; generate returned **7 entries** for a
40-minute beginner — including **Seated Leg Curl listed twice** (positions 2
and 6, the second as "Second lighter round… repeats setup") and a "Treadmill
**Run**" whose notes prescribe an easy **walk**. The revise fixed the count
(4 machines) once she pushed back.
**Engineering read:** no post-processing guard on the generated draft — no
uniqueness check on exercise names, no exercise-count ceiling derived from
experience level/time, and catalog grounding happily maps a "second round" to
a duplicate entry instead of extra sets.

### 4. CONFIRMED — The coach validates false premises about its own routine (S2, Frank)
> Frank asked to cut "three sets of ten on squats" and "the leg extensions" —
> **neither existed** in the routine it had just generated (percent-based
> singles + good mornings).

Evidence: the revise reply played along: "No leg extensions, no unnecessary
volume… respects your knee history," and the changeSummary implies those
things were removed. A 30-year lifter testing the coach would catch this
instantly; it converts authority into sycophancy.
**Engineering read:** the revise prompt optimizes for agreement — nothing
instructs the model to diff the user's claim against the actual current
routine and (politely) correct a false premise; changeSummary is generated
from the conversation, not from an actual before/after diff.

### 5. CONFIRMED — e1RM estimates quoted back as performed lifts (S2, Frank)
> Coach follow-up: "You've consistently hit **375–385 for your top single**
> for three weeks running, and your estimated 1RM crept from 377 up to 391."

Evidence: the attached digest shows top singles of 355 → 365 → 375; "385" and
"391" are **e1RM values from 5-rep sets** (330×5, 335×5). The trend statement
was directionally right, the numbers were mislabeled — to the exact user
archetype who knows his singles to the pound.
**Engineering read:** digest lines clearly label `e1RM`, but the prompt never
tells the model e1RM ≠ a lift that happened; it blends `bestSets` and PR lines
into one narrative. Cheap fix: one instruction line ("e1RM values are
estimates, never claim them as performed sets").

### PERSONA-OPINION (logged, not filed as defects)
- **Priya, "kettlebell swings unsafe for my back"** — her back issue exists
  only in her private lore; her saved limitations say "no jumping/quiet only"
  and the plan honored both. (Her later painNote "lower back tight after
  swings" DID reach the digest — a proactive M4 loop could catch this one too.)
- **Tyler, "floor press is settling for less" + "triceps volume hurts my
  elbows"** — the substitution and the extra triceps work are both things he
  explicitly asked for; no elbow signal existed in his digest (0 pain notes).
  Expectation management, not a bug.
- **Frank, "good mornings are fluff"** — defensible accessory programming;
  the app matched his percentages/RPE style everywhere else. Taste.
- **Dana, "no feedback on my plank"** — she wants coaching from the voice
  *parser*; wrong surface today, but a fair hint that import screens could
  hand off to the coach.
- **Frank, residual hedging ("if knees still feel okay")** — the coach gave
  him a number and a week as demanded; the hedge is a safety feature, not a bug.

### Engineering-found during evidence review (no persona flagged these)
- **Cable exercise prescribed to a cable-less home gym (Marcus).** Generate
  AND revise kept "Cable Triceps Pushdown" with the note "Substitute with a
  band… if you don't have a cable machine" — his equipment list (no cable) was
  in the request both times. The model knows it's unsure and hedges in prose
  instead of just prescribing the band movement.
- **Voice parse discards every weight by schema design.** "the sixteen kilo…
  the pin was at ninety… seventies for eight" all vanish —
  `voiceWorkoutParseSchema` has no weight field (`sourceText/name/setCount/
  repText/confidence` only). Nobody can log loads by voice.

## What the personas LIKED (do not break these)
- **Revise obeys constraints fast** — every persona's edit was honored in one
  round: incline press → chest-supported row (Marcus), 7 → 4 common machines
  (Dana), 16 → 8 sets (Priya), bench → dumbbell/cable backups (Tyler), 3×10 →
  RPE-8 single + 80% backoffs (Frank).
- **Memory-informed follow-ups landed 5/5** — Priya got "officially plan for
  2 sessions/week" from her real attendance; Tyler's "too easy" pattern was
  named; Frank got a number and a week (390, week 4); Dana got a
  confidence-scaled "+5 lb on ONE exercise"; Marcus got the volume cut.
- **Coach edits saved routines properly** — `updated_draft` +
  `editedRoutineID` fired for Marcus/Priya/Tyler; Priya's 15-minute option was
  written into the routine notes exactly as asked.
- **Tone calibration** — Dana called the RPE explanation "clear… without
  jargon"; Frank called the paused-vs-pin answer "exactly what I wanted: no
  hedging".

## Framework quirks (for the next sweep)
- Personas occasionally cite their **own words** as "evidence" quotes
  (Dana's plank complaint) — the runner's digest labels app output clearly,
  but the mini model still slips; treat quotes as pointers, verify in the log.
- The triage model **recycled likes into S3 "issues"** (4 of its 16 issues
  were praise) — harmless but pad; deterministic post-filter or a schema
  `sentiment` field would fix it.
- Scripted edit prompts can carry **false premises** relative to what was
  actually generated (Frank's "3×10 squats") — accidentally our most
  interesting probe; worth keeping one deliberate false-premise edit per
  sweep.
- summary.json cost accounting missed the sweep's first `[usage]` line
  (byte-vs-char offset bug, fixed in the runner; this report's numbers are
  corrected).
